# Digital Banking Product Manager Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

Yes, although from the Digital Banking side I think the problem is a little broader than complaint reporting.

We receive a lot of information about customer problems that never becomes a formal complaint. Customers leave app-store reviews, submit feedback through online banking, contact Customer Service, report problems to branches, and sometimes respond to surveys.

For my team, the important question is usually, "What problems are customers experiencing with our digital products?" Whether someone formally classified the interaction as a complaint is secondary.

I agree that leadership doesn't have a good way to connect those different signals today.

### 2. Can you walk me through the customer feedback Digital Banking currently receives and how you use it?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

We primarily look at app-store reviews and feedback submitted through our digital banking channels.

App-store reviews are public and usually anonymous from the bank's perspective. We can see the rating, review text, date, app version in some cases, and which platform it came from. We can't reliably connect that review to a bank customer.

Feedback submitted while someone is authenticated in online or mobile banking can contain more context. Depending on how it was submitted, we may know which customer provided it and have additional technical information.

My team reviews that information for recurring problems and significant issues. If we see several customers talking about the same thing, we'll investigate whether there may be a product defect or usability problem.

We also hear about digital issues from Customer Service. If they're suddenly receiving a lot of calls about something like login failures, they'll usually let us know.

### 3. How do you determine when feedback requires action?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

Volume is one factor, but it's not the only one.

If we get 20 reviews about the same problem, that's obviously something we'd investigate. But a single report can also be important if it describes something severe, like a customer being unable to access their account or a feature exposing information incorrectly.

We look at things like frequency, severity, customer impact, whether we can reproduce the problem, and whether it's increasing.

The difficulty is that we don't have a formal scoring model that combines all of those factors. A lot of it relies on the product team recognizing patterns and using judgment.

### 4. Do you currently distinguish between customer feedback and customer complaints?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

Not very formally.

We tend to call most of what we receive "feedback" because our systems aren't really designed around complaint case management.

If someone submits feedback through online banking that clearly describes a complaint about how the bank handled something, we may send it to Customer Service.

But I wouldn't say we're systematically reviewing every piece of digital feedback and deciding whether it meets an enterprise complaint definition. We don't have an enterprise definition to apply.

That's part of why I think the terminology discussion matters.

From my perspective, a one-star app review saying, "I've been unable to deposit checks for three days and nobody will help me," certainly sounds like a customer complaint. The fact that I can't identify the customer doesn't make the dissatisfaction less real.

It does change what we can do with it.

### 5. Customer Service raised a distinction between a complaint and a complaint that can be managed as an individual Customer Service case. Does that distinction make sense to you?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

Yes. That distinction actually fits how I think about our data much better.

A lot of what we have could represent complaints without being complaint cases.

The problem I would have with defining a complaint based on whether Customer Service can create a case is that we'd exclude a significant amount of digital customer experience data.

At the same time, I don't think every one-star review needs to generate a Customer Service case. That would create a huge amount of work without necessarily helping the customer.

I think we need a way to preserve the feedback as a signal while still identifying which interactions need individual case management.

### 6. Are there situations where the same customer issue could appear in both Digital Banking data and Customer Service complaint data?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

Absolutely, and we have no reliable way to identify that today.

A customer might leave an app-store review because mobile deposit isn't working and then call Customer Service about the same problem.

Or 50 customers might contact Customer Service about an outage while another 30 leave negative reviews. That's potentially one underlying product incident generating 80 customer signals.

If we simply combined those records and called them 80 complaints, that might be technically true under one definition, but it could also give leadership the wrong impression about what's happening.

On the other hand, collapsing all 80 into one "issue" would hide the scale of the customer impact.

Ideally, we'd be able to understand both: **how many customer signals we received and what underlying issue or issues they relate to.**

### 7. What information is available in your current digital feedback data?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

It varies by source.

For app-store reviews, we generally have:

- Review date
- Star rating
- Review text
- Platform
- App version when available
- Sometimes information about the device or operating system

For authenticated digital feedback, we may also have:

- Customer identifier
- Date and time
- Feedback text
- Digital channel
- Page or feature associated with the feedback
- Session or technical information
- Device information

The structure isn't consistent between the sources.

We also don't have standardized complaint categories that match Customer Service. Digital Banking may describe something as "authentication," "mobile deposit," or "performance," while Customer Service may categorize the same customer experience differently.

### 8. How do digital customer issues currently make their way to other teams?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

It's fairly informal.

If we identify a technical issue, we'll usually work with the appropriate technology team and create work in our normal product or development process.

If something appears to be an individual customer service issue, we can send it to Customer Service.

If we see something that looks like it could have regulatory implications, we'd involve Compliance.

Customer Service also contacts us when they're seeing a spike in digital complaints.

The problem is that those handoffs don't necessarily create a consistent relationship between the original customer feedback and whatever happens afterward. Once something becomes a development ticket, Customer Service complaint, or Compliance case, it's difficult to trace everything back to the same underlying problem.

### 9. What are the biggest pain points with the current process?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

The biggest one is fragmentation.

We're looking at several sources separately, and Customer Service and Compliance have additional information that we don't routinely see.

It's also difficult to quantify customer impact. We might know that a technical incident happened, but connecting that incident to all of the feedback, calls, complaints, and reviews it generated requires manual investigation.

Categorization is another problem because different teams describe the same issues differently.

And there's a lot of manual pattern recognition. Someone has to notice that several comments are describing the same thing.

I don't necessarily need every source consolidated into one operational system, but I would like a better way to connect the information.

### 10. If a future solution included Digital Banking feedback, what would you want it to preserve?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

I wouldn't want us to lose the original customer language. Categories are useful, but sometimes the actual feedback tells you something that the category doesn't.

I'd also want to preserve the source. Ten Customer Service complaints and ten one-star app reviews are different kinds of signals even if they're related to the same issue.

Technical context is important when it's available, especially things like app version, platform, feature, or device information.

And I'd want us to distinguish between the individual customer signal and the underlying problem. Those are both valuable things to measure.

### 11. What would success look like from your perspective?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

I'd like to be able to recognize emerging digital problems earlier and understand their customer impact without manually checking several places.

If there's a mobile banking issue, I want to be able to see that we're getting increased app-store complaints, Customer Service contacts, and authenticated feedback around the same problem.

I'd also like leadership to understand the difference between an increase in customer feedback and an increase in distinct underlying problems.

From an operational standpoint, I'd want clearer handoffs when something needs to become a Customer Service or Compliance case.

And I don't want Digital Banking employees manually entering information into another system just to support reporting.

### 12. Is there anything else you think I should know or investigate?

**Digital Banking Product Manager:**

I would look at whether we can connect customer signals to known incidents or product issues.

Right now, we're talking a lot about connecting complaint records across departments, but there's another relationship that may matter: connecting those complaints to the thing that actually caused them.

For example, if a mobile release causes login failures, leadership may care that we received 75 pieces of negative feedback, but they also need to understand that those 75 signals were associated with one known incident.

I'd also be careful about relying too heavily on counts from public feedback sources. App-store reviews don't represent all customers, and people who leave reviews may not be representative of the broader customer population.

But they can still be valuable as an early signal, especially when combined with information from other channels.
