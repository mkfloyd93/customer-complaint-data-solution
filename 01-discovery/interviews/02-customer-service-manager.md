# Customer Service Manager Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Yes, although I would clarify that I don't necessarily think our complaint numbers are wrong. I think the different groups are measuring different things.

For Customer Service, we generally consider something a complaint when a customer expresses dissatisfaction about a bank product, service, employee interaction, or experience and expects some kind of response or resolution.

There is still judgment involved, though. One representative might record a customer being upset about a fee as a complaint, while another might resolve the fee issue during the call and not create a complaint record.

So I agree that we don't have a consistent enterprise view, but part of the problem is understanding what each number actually represents.

### 2. Can you walk me through the current complaint process in Customer Service?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Complaints can come into Customer Service through several channels. We receive them directly through phone calls, secure messages, and emails. Branches and other departments can also refer customers or issues to us.

When a representative identifies something as a complaint, they create a record in our Customer Service system. They select a category, enter notes describing the issue, associate the customer and product when applicable, and capture information such as the contact channel and branch.

The representative will try to resolve the complaint themselves when possible. If they can't, they may refer it to another department or escalate it to a supervisor or Compliance depending on the issue.

Once the issue has been addressed, the complaint is marked as resolved.

### 3. What information is captured about a complaint?

**Customer Service Manager:**

We capture the customer when they're identifiable, the complaint category, description or notes, contact channel, related product when applicable, branch information when relevant, and status.

Our categories include things like fees, account servicing, employee or service concerns, online and mobile banking, cards, lending, and a few others. There's also an "Other" category.

We don't currently have a formal severity field.

If something appears to require Compliance involvement, the representative or supervisor escalates it rather than assigning a Compliance severity within our system.

### 4. How are complaints categorized, and what business rules do representatives follow?

**Customer Service Manager:**

We have general guidance and training, but I wouldn't say we have a detailed decision tree that tells a representative exactly what to do in every situation.

Some issues clearly need to be escalated to Compliance. Things involving potential discrimination, employee fraud, unfair lending practices, regulatory concerns, or similar allegations should be escalated. Legal threats would generally be escalated as well.

For normal complaint categorization, representatives use their judgment based on the customer's primary issue.

One limitation is that the system only allows one primary complaint category. If someone complains about multiple things during the same interaction, the representative has to decide which one best represents the complaint.

That can create inconsistency.

### 5. How is the monthly Customer Service complaint report created?

**Customer Service Manager:**

A supervisor exports complaint information from the Customer Service system into Excel each month.

There is usually some cleanup involved before we can use it. We review categories, look for duplicates, investigate incomplete records, and sometimes go back to representatives if we don't understand what happened based on the notes.

Then we create totals and breakdowns by things like complaint category, contact channel, branch, and resolution status. We also look at trends and add commentary when something stands out.

It takes several hours each month.

If leadership has questions about differences between our report and Compliance's report, we may also have to manually compare information between the two.

### 6. What are the biggest pain points with the current complaint process?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Consistency is probably the biggest one.

Representatives receive training, but there is still judgment involved in deciding what gets recorded and how it gets categorized.

"Other" gets used more than I'd like because sometimes the existing categories don't feel like a good fit or representatives aren't sure which category to choose.

The quality of the descriptions also varies. Some representatives write very detailed notes, while others provide only enough information to understand the immediate issue.

Another problem is that complaints may not get recorded if the representative resolves the issue immediately. They're focused on helping the customer, so sometimes creating a separate complaint record doesn't happen.

Branch referrals are inconsistent too. Some branches provide everything we need, while others send very limited information.

And then there's the manual reconciliation with Compliance when our numbers don't match.

### 7. How are repeat contacts or multiple complaints from the same customer handled?

**Customer Service Manager:**

We count complaint records, not unique customers.

If a customer contacts us multiple times, it depends on how the representative handles it. Sometimes they update the existing complaint, and sometimes another complaint record gets created.

If the customer contacts us through another channel or speaks with a different representative, it's more likely that a second record could be created.

That's one of the reasons I wouldn't assume that a complaint count always equals a count of unique customer issues.

### 8. What would you want a future solution to improve?

**Customer Service Manager:**

I'd like clearer definitions and more consistent application of them.

I'd also like to reduce the amount of manual cleanup and reporting work we do every month.

It would be helpful to identify patterns earlier instead of waiting until we're preparing the monthly report to realize that we're seeing the same issue repeatedly.

I don't expect one number to replace every department's reporting. Compliance has different responsibilities than we do, and Digital Banking may be looking at different kinds of feedback.

What I do want is a way to understand how those things relate so that we're not constantly debating which number is correct.

And I don't want the solution to require representatives to enter the same information into another system.

### 9. What would success look like from your perspective?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Success would mean that everyone understands what we're counting and why.

For Customer Service specifically, I'd want representatives to have clearer guidance so the same type of customer issue is handled consistently.

I'd want less manual cleanup and reporting work for supervisors.

I'd also want the information we're collecting to actually lead to action. If we're seeing the same problem repeatedly, someone should be able to recognize that and address the underlying issue.

I don't think success means forcing every department to report the exact same number. It means understanding the relationship between the numbers and being able to trust the information.

### 10. Is there anything else you think I should know or investigate?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Training will need to be considered if definitions or processes change. Changing a dashboard without changing how representatives capture information won't solve the underlying consistency problem.

I'd also talk directly to representatives because they'll be able to tell you where the process is confusing or doesn't work well in practice.

And I wouldn't assume that every piece of negative customer feedback needs to become a Customer Service complaint record. There are different kinds of feedback, and we need to understand what we're actually trying to measure before changing the process.

## Follow-Up Discussion: Negative Feedback and Complaint Definitions

### How should negative app-store feedback be treated?

**Customer Service Manager:**

I think it's valuable information, but I wouldn't automatically treat every negative app-store review as a Customer Service complaint.

For example, if someone posts a review saying, "The app keeps crashing," that's clearly negative feedback and something Digital Banking should care about. But we don't know who the customer is, we can't associate it with a customer record, and we don't have an individual case that we can respond to and resolve through our current complaint process.

If a customer calls us and says the mobile app keeps crashing and they need help, that can become a complaint record because we have an identifiable customer and an issue we can work through.

That doesn't mean the app-store reviews aren't important. If 20 people suddenly start saying the app is crashing, that's a significant signal even if there aren't 20 Customer Service complaint records.

Leadership may need visibility into both, but I wouldn't assume they're equivalent.

### Can negative feedback become a complaint?

**Customer Service Manager:**

Yes.

For example, feedback submitted through authenticated online banking may identify the customer and describe a specific problem. That could be referred to Customer Service and become a complaint case.

Anonymous app-store reviews are harder because we can't follow up with the individual customer.

But even if something doesn't become an individual complaint case, it can still identify a systemic problem that the bank needs to investigate.

The biggest thing I would want to avoid is counting the same underlying issue multiple times just because it appears through several channels.

### It sounds like a customer could express dissatisfaction about the mobile app through a negative review, which seems to align with the definition you gave earlier. Does the differentiation come down to not knowing which customer submitted it? Why does not knowing the customer change whether it's considered a complaint?

**Customer Service Manager:**

That's a fair point, and I think it exposes a weakness in the way I described our definition.

If we're simply saying that a complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction about a bank product, service, or experience, then yes, a negative app review could absolutely meet that definition.

The part about expecting a response or resolution is probably influenced by the way Customer Service operationalizes complaints. Our system is designed around individual cases. We associate the complaint with a customer when possible, document what happened, assign or escalate it, and eventually mark it resolved.

Knowing who the customer is matters to that workflow because it allows us to manage the issue as an individual case. But that doesn't necessarily mean something stops being a complaint just because we don't know who submitted it.

An anonymous complaint might need to be resolved at the product or systemic level rather than at the individual customer level.

So I think there may actually be two different questions here:

1. What constitutes a complaint?
2. What constitutes a complaint that Customer Service can manage as an individual case?

I wouldn't want Customer Service's current workflow or system limitations to define the bank's enterprise definition of a complaint.

That's something I'd want Digital Banking, Compliance, and Data Governance involved in before we tried to standardize it.
