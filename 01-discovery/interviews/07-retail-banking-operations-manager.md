# Retail Banking Operations Manager Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

Yes. From the branch perspective, I think the inconsistency is real, although I wouldn't say branches think of themselves as part of a formal complaint-reporting process most of the time.

Customers complain to branch employees every day about fees, wait times, policies, digital banking, lending decisions, and other issues. Some of those are handled immediately in the branch. Others get referred to Customer Service or another department.

The challenge is that employees don't always have a clear understanding of which interactions need to become formal complaint records.

I also don't think branch managers have particularly good visibility into complaint trends today. They generally hear about issues happening in their own branch, but it's difficult to know whether something they're seeing is isolated or part of a larger pattern across the bank.

### 2. Can you walk me through what happens when a customer complains in a branch?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

Usually the employee or branch manager tries to resolve the issue while the customer is there.

If it's something they can handle, such as explaining a policy or correcting a straightforward service issue, they may resolve it without involving anyone else.

If they can't resolve it, they might contact Customer Service, another operational team, or a manager depending on the issue.

For more serious concerns, employees are expected to escalate them appropriately, including to Compliance when necessary.

The exact process varies somewhat based on the type of complaint and the employee's experience.

### 3. We've heard that branch referrals to Customer Service sometimes contain limited information. Why does that happen?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

I can believe that.

Branch employees are usually focused on the customer standing in front of them. If they need Customer Service to take over, they may send enough information to get the customer connected but not necessarily document everything Customer Service would want for a complaint record.

There also isn't one consistent referral format used for every situation.

An experienced branch manager may provide a detailed explanation, while another employee might simply say that the customer is upset and needs assistance.

I don't think that's because people don't care about documentation. It's more that the process hasn't been designed around creating consistent complaint data.

### 4. How do branch employees decide when an issue should be formally recorded or escalated?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

There is guidance for certain types of issues, particularly things that need Compliance involvement, but there's still judgment involved.

Obvious serious allegations should be escalated.

For more general service complaints, employees may think of the issue as resolved if they were able to satisfy the customer in the branch.

That's probably similar to what you're hearing from Customer Service. If the employee solved the problem, their immediate goal has been achieved, so creating a complaint record afterward may not feel necessary.

If we decide that those interactions need to be captured more consistently, we'd need very clear criteria and a process that doesn't create a lot of additional work.

### 5. Are there particular challenges with complaints that involve multiple areas of the bank?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

Yes. Customers don't experience the bank according to our organizational structure.

A customer might come into a branch because they can't access mobile banking, then become frustrated with how long it takes us to get help from another department, and also complain about a fee that resulted from the original problem.

From the customer's perspective, that's one bad experience.

Internally, it could involve Digital Banking, Customer Service, the branch, and potentially another operational team.

It's not always obvious who should own the complaint or how it should be categorized.

### 6. What complaint information would be useful to branch managers?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

I'd want branch managers to understand what's happening in their own location.

Things like:

- Complaint volume and trends
- Common complaint topics
- Repeat or recurring issues
- Service-related concerns
- Whether issues are being resolved
- How their branch compares with broader patterns

The comparison piece needs to be handled carefully, though. A branch with more customers or more complex transactions may naturally have more complaints.

I wouldn't want a simple complaint count turned into a branch performance ranking without considering context.

It would also be useful for a manager to know when something they're seeing locally is actually happening across the bank. If five branches are suddenly getting complaints about the same policy or system, that's very different from one branch having an isolated service problem.

### 7. If the complaint process changed, what concerns would you have for branch employees?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

The biggest concern would be adding another administrative process.

Branch employees already have significant documentation, operational, customer service, and compliance responsibilities.

If we're asking them to capture additional complaint information, it needs to be clear when they should do it and why.

Ideally, we would use information that already exists rather than asking employees to enter the same information multiple times.

Training would also be important. If we expect more consistent complaint identification across branches, employees need examples that reflect situations they actually encounter.

### 8. What would success look like from your perspective?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

Branch employees would have clear guidance about when something needs to be captured or escalated.

The process wouldn't interfere significantly with serving customers.

Branch managers would have useful visibility into the issues affecting their customers without having to manually track complaints themselves.

And we'd be able to distinguish between problems a branch can address locally and problems that require action somewhere else in the organization.

I'd also want branch managers to receive enough context to use the information constructively rather than simply being told that their branch had a certain number of complaints.

### 9. Is there anything else you think I should know or investigate?

**Retail Banking Operations Manager:**

I'd make sure you understand how complaint volume relates to branch activity before creating branch comparisons.

I'd also look at whether branch employees need a better referral process rather than simply more training. If the current process makes it difficult to provide Customer Service with the information they need, training alone won't solve that.

And I'd keep the customer's perspective in mind. Internally, we may see several departments and systems, but the customer often sees one experience with the bank.
