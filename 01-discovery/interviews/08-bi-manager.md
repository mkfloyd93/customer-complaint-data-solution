# BI Manager Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

**BI Manager:**

Yes. From the BI side, the main thing I'd add is that this isn't just a reporting problem.

We've built reports from some of these sources before, but reporting can only be as consistent as the underlying data and definitions.

If Customer Service, Compliance, and Digital Banking are capturing different concepts in different systems, Power BI isn't going to resolve those differences automatically. We need to understand what the records represent and how they relate before we decide how to model them.

I do think there's an opportunity to reduce the manual reporting that's happening today and provide better enterprise visibility, but we'd need to address some of the underlying data issues as part of that.

### 2. Where does the relevant data currently live?

**BI Manager:**

At a high level, we're dealing with several different sources.

Customer Service complaint information is stored in the Customer Service application. That data is available to our data team through a database extract.

Compliance uses a separate vendor application. We currently receive a scheduled extract from that system for some compliance reporting, although access to the more sensitive fields is restricted.

Digital Banking is more fragmented. Authenticated feedback is stored within our digital platform and can be extracted. App-store review data isn't currently part of our internal data environment. The Digital Banking team mostly reviews that through the app platforms and some manual reporting.

Branch complaint information is less of a separate data source. If a branch refers something to Customer Service and a complaint is created, it appears in the Customer Service data. If the branch resolves something locally and doesn't create a complaint record, there isn't necessarily a structured complaint record for us to report from.

There are also other enterprise sources that could potentially help provide context, such as customer, product, branch, and organizational reference data.

### 3. What does the current data and reporting architecture look like?

**BI Manager:**

We have a central analytics environment where data from several operational systems is brought in for reporting.

Our general pattern is to extract data from source systems, transform it into reporting-friendly structures, and then expose curated datasets or semantic models to Power BI.

We try not to build important enterprise reports directly against operational systems.

Some of the Customer Service and Compliance data is already available in the analytics environment because those teams have existing reporting needs, but they haven't been modeled together.

Digital feedback would require some additional work because not all of those sources are currently ingested.

We also have standard enterprise dimensions for things like dates, branches, products, and some customer information that we'd want to reuse where appropriate rather than redefining them specifically for this project.

### 4. What existing complaint reporting or data products already exist?

**BI Manager:**

Customer Service's primary monthly complaint report is still Excel-based. The source data comes from their system, but the final cleanup, calculations, and commentary are largely manual.

Compliance has some existing reporting supported by our analytics environment, but much of their operational complaint reporting is still managed within their team because of the specialized requirements and access restrictions.

We've built Power BI reports that include customer service and operational metrics, but there isn't currently an enterprise complaint model that combines these different sources.

Digital Banking has product analytics, but their customer feedback isn't integrated with Customer Service complaint reporting.

So we wouldn't be starting from zero, but we'd need to understand which existing assets we can reuse.

### 5. What data-quality or integration issues do you already know about?

**BI Manager:**

The lack of a shared identifier between systems is probably the biggest integration problem based on what you've described.

If a Customer Service complaint becomes a Compliance case, we don't have a consistently populated key that connects those records.

For existing historical data, we could potentially try to identify likely relationships using customer identifiers, dates, categories, or other attributes, but that would be an inferred match rather than a guaranteed relationship.

We'd need to be very careful about presenting that as authoritative.

Customer Service categorization is another concern. If categories are applied inconsistently at the source, we can map or standardize them for reporting, but that doesn't necessarily fix the underlying data-quality problem.

Free-text fields also vary considerably in quality, which limits how reliably we can use them without additional processing.

And, as you've already heard, some customer complaints may not exist in the structured data at all.

### 6. From a technical perspective, how could we connect information from the different systems?

**BI Manager:**

There are a few possible approaches, and I wouldn't choose one until we've profiled the data.

For reporting purposes, we don't necessarily need to integrate the operational systems directly with one another.

We could bring the relevant data into the analytics environment and model the relationships there.

If we're able to establish common enterprise concepts and mappings, we could preserve each source record while creating shared dimensions or relationship tables that allow us to analyze them together.

For future records, I'd strongly prefer that operational handoffs preserve identifiers. If a Customer Service complaint is escalated and creates a Compliance case, capturing that relationship at the time of the handoff would be much more reliable than trying to reconstruct it later.

For historical data, we may need a different strategy because those identifiers don't exist.

### 7. We've heard that multiple customer signals can relate to the same underlying issue or incident. Do we currently have data that could support that relationship?

**BI Manager:**

Possibly, but not consistently.

Technology teams already track incidents and defects in other systems. In some cases, Customer Service or Digital Banking may reference an incident or work item in their notes, but I don't believe that's standardized.

If connecting complaints to underlying incidents becomes an important requirement, we'd need to look at those systems and determine whether there is a practical way to establish that relationship.

I wouldn't assume we can reliably do that historically.

Going forward, it might be possible to create a more structured relationship between known incidents and customer feedback.

That's probably something I'd treat as a potential later capability unless data discovery shows that the existing relationships are better than we expect.

### 8. Are there technical or platform constraints we should consider?

**BI Manager:**

Access and security will be important, particularly with Compliance data and customer-identifiable information.

We'd likely want to minimize sensitive information in the reporting model and use role-based access where more detailed information is necessary.

Performance and maintainability matter too. I wouldn't want to create a model that requires complicated logic every month just to reconcile records.

We should also avoid putting business rules only inside Power BI. If a definition or mapping is important to multiple reports, it should ideally be managed in the data layer or another governed location rather than recreated independently in individual dashboards.

For external sources like app-store reviews, we'd also need to determine a sustainable ingestion method. A manual spreadsheet upload might work for a prototype, but I wouldn't recommend it as the long-term production approach.

### 9. What would you want to investigate during data discovery before recommending a solution?

**BI Manager:**

I'd want actual samples or extracts from each relevant source.

For Customer Service, I'd profile:

- Record counts and trends
- Category values and usage
- Missing fields
- Duplicate or near-duplicate records
- Repeat contacts
- Customer and product identifiers
- Branch information
- Status and resolution dates
- Free-text quality

For Compliance, I'd want to understand:

- Which fields are available for analytics
- How cases are categorized
- Customer identifiers
- Dates and statuses
- How often Customer Service complaint IDs appear in the notes or other fields
- Access restrictions

For Digital Banking, I'd want to understand the structure and volume of each feedback source and how much identifying or technical information is available.

I'd also compare the available enterprise reference data for customers, products, branches, and dates to determine what can be standardized through existing dimensions.

Only after that would I feel comfortable recommending the target data model.

### 10. What would success look like from the BI team's perspective?

**BI Manager:**

We'd have a maintainable data product rather than a Power BI report held together by manual cleanup.

The business definitions and mappings would be documented and owned outside the dashboard.

Each source would retain its appropriate meaning, but users could analyze related information together.

Refreshes would be automated wherever practical.

Access controls would be appropriate for the sensitivity of the data.

And the reporting layer would make it difficult for users to accidentally compare numbers that mean different things.

I'd also want enough documentation that another analyst could understand how the model works without needing the original project team to explain it.

### 11. Is there anything else you think I should know or investigate?

**BI Manager:**

I'd avoid trying to solve every operational problem before we build anything.

Some improvements, like creating shared identifiers during future handoffs, may require changes to operational systems and could take time.

That doesn't necessarily prevent us from delivering an initial reporting capability using the data we have, as long as we're transparent about its limitations.

I would separate what we need for a reliable first version from longer-term improvements.

I'd also recommend that the next step be hands-on data profiling rather than more assumptions about the data. We've learned a lot from the stakeholders, but now we need to see whether the data supports what we've been told.
