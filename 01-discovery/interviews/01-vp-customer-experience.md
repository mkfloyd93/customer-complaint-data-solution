# VP of Customer Experience Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. What prompted this request?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

This really came to a head during a recent leadership meeting. Customer Service's monthly reporting showed that complaints had been trending upward for about six months, but Compliance's reporting didn't show nearly the same increase. At the same time, Digital Banking had been telling us they were seeing more negative feedback, particularly related to the mobile app.

When we started asking questions, no one could clearly explain how those things related to each other. We weren't sure whether we were seeing one problem represented in different ways or completely different issues.

The immediate problem is that I don't feel like we have a reliable, consistent view of what customers are experiencing. We also spend too much time trying to reconcile reports when leadership asks questions about the numbers.

### 2. What information do you have today? Can you walk me through the reports or sources you're currently using and where you're seeing inconsistencies?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

Customer Service prepares a monthly Excel report. It includes things like complaint counts, categories, contact channels, branch information, and whether the complaint was resolved. My understanding is that their managers compile that from the Customer Service system.

Compliance has its own Excel reporting. They're focused more specifically on complaints that may have regulatory implications. They track things like regulatory categories, when the complaint was received and resolved, and whether it required escalation.

Digital Banking is different. They monitor app-store reviews and feedback submitted through online banking, but they don't produce the same type of monthly complaint report.

Branches also receive complaints directly. Significant issues are supposed to make their way into the Customer Service system, but I don't know how consistently that's happening.

The biggest issue is that the numbers don't line up. We don't have a clear way to tell whether that's because the departments are counting different things, because information is missing, or because the same issue is showing up in multiple places.

### 3. How is this information used today? What decisions or actions does it support?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

Customer Service uses its report to review complaint activity each month. Compliance uses its information separately for regulatory and risk reporting. Digital Banking will raise issues when they notice something significant in customer feedback.

From my perspective, we're mostly reactive.

If something looks concerning, someone starts asking questions and the teams dig into it. We don't have consistent thresholds or a shared process for deciding when a trend deserves further investigation.

There's also a fair amount of manual effort involved in creating the reports and then reconciling them when leadership has questions.

### 4. What do you need to understand that you can't reliably understand today? What are the most important questions you want the eventual solution to answer?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

At a basic level, I want to know whether customer complaints are getting better or worse.

Beyond that, I'd want to understand:

* What customers are complaining about
* Where complaints are coming from
* Which products, channels, branches, or areas of the bank are generating problems
* How quickly complaints are being resolved
* Whether we're seeing the same problems repeatedly
* Whether certain issues are more severe or create regulatory or reputational risk
* Whether there are emerging problems we should investigate before they become larger issues

I don't necessarily expect every department to use the information in exactly the same way, but leadership needs a reliable overall picture.

### 5. Who needs this information, and how do you expect their needs to differ?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

Senior leadership needs the overall view. They shouldn't have to dig through individual complaint records to understand what's happening.

Customer Experience, Customer Service, Compliance, Retail Banking, and Digital Banking leadership will probably need more detail because they're responsible for understanding and responding to the issues.

Operational teams may need to drill further into specific complaints or trends.

Branch managers may need visibility into complaints related to their own branches, but I wouldn't expect them to have access to everything across the organization.

Compliance may also have information that needs to be handled differently because of the sensitivity of some of their cases.

### 6. What would success look like to you?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

The biggest thing would be trust.

If we present complaint information in a leadership meeting, I want people to trust that the numbers are accurate and understand what they mean instead of spending the meeting debating whose spreadsheet is correct.

I'd also like to reduce the amount of manual work involved in compiling and reconciling these reports.

But I don't want us to build a dashboard that no one uses. The information needs to help us identify meaningful customer experience problems and decide where we need to take action.

Ideally, we'd also identify emerging issues earlier instead of waiting until they become obvious enough that someone raises a concern.

### 7. Who else should be involved in discovery?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

Definitely the Customer Service Manager because that team owns most of the current complaint process.

I'd also talk to one of the senior Customer Service representatives who actually works with the complaints. The process on paper isn't always exactly what happens day to day.

The Compliance Manager needs to be involved because their definition and reporting requirements may be different.

The Digital Banking Product Manager can explain how they're currently collecting and using digital feedback.

I'd include Retail Banking Operations so we understand what's happening in the branches.

We should also involve the BI Manager once we start getting into the existing reporting and data environment.

And I'd talk to our Data Governance Lead. We've tried to standardize some of this before, and one of the problems was that departments couldn't agree on what actually counts as a complaint.

Information Security will probably need to be involved as well once we better understand what customer information we'd be using and who needs access to it.

### 8. Is there anything else I should know before moving forward?

**VP of Customer Experience:**

The biggest thing is that I don't want us to repeat the previous standardization effort.

We spent a lot of time trying to get everyone to agree on complaint reporting, and it stalled partly because different groups use the word "complaint" differently. I don't want us to just pick one department's definition and tell everyone else to use it.

There is also sensitive customer information involved, so we'll need Compliance, Data Governance, and eventually Information Security involved in decisions about the data and access.

I also don't want the solution to create duplicate work. If the answer requires employees to enter the same complaint into multiple systems or maintain another spreadsheet, I don't think it will be sustainable.

I'd like us to make progress this quarter, but I'd rather understand the problem correctly than rush into building something and have to redo it later.
