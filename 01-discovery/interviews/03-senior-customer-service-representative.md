# Senior Customer Service Representative Interview

> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder interview created for a portfolio project. The stakeholder responses below were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I developed the discovery approach and questions and used the simulated responses as source material for my analysis. Findings and project decisions based on these interviews are documented separately in the [Discovery Findings](../03-discovery-findings.md).

## Interview

### 1. Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

Yes. From my perspective, the biggest issue is consistency.

Even within Customer Service, complaints aren't always handled the same way. We have guidance and training, but there are still situations where the representative has to make a judgment call.

I also don't really know how Compliance defines a complaint. I know there are certain things we're supposed to escalate to them, but once something goes to Compliance, we don't have much visibility into what happens with it.

The manual reporting part sounds accurate too. Sometimes supervisors come back to us when they're preparing reports because they need help understanding an older complaint record or what we meant in the notes.

### 2. Can you walk me through what you actually do when a customer complains?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

My first priority is usually trying to resolve whatever problem the customer is having.

If I recognize it as a complaint, I'll create a complaint record in the system. I'll choose a category, document what happened, associate the customer, and fill in the other information we need.

If I can fix the problem myself, I do that. If I can't, I may need to send it to another team, involve a supervisor, or escalate it depending on what the issue is.

Once everything has been addressed, the complaint can be marked as resolved.

### 3. How do you decide whether something should be recorded as a complaint?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

That's not always completely clear.

There are obvious situations. If someone calls and says they're angry because they were charged a fee and they want it fixed, I'd consider that a complaint.

But customers say negative things all the time while they're calling about something else. Someone might say, "I hate the new website," while I'm helping them reset their password. I wouldn't necessarily create a complaint record every time someone makes a comment like that.

I think about how serious the issue is and whether there's something we actually need to address.

There's also the practical side of it. Sometimes I solve the customer's problem during the call, and once they're happy, I might not think about going back and creating a separate complaint record. You're trying to finish your documentation and get ready for the next customer.

Different representatives probably make those decisions a little differently.

### 4. How do you choose a complaint category?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

Most of the time it's straightforward, but there are definitely situations where it's not.

The system only lets us choose one primary category. If someone has more than one issue, we have to decide which one is the main complaint.

For example, maybe someone is upset because their debit card didn't work, but they're also really unhappy with how they were treated when they called about it previously. Is that a card complaint or a service complaint?

Or maybe a mobile banking issue caused them to get charged a fee. One representative might categorize that under mobile banking and another might choose fees.

If none of the categories really fit, we use "Other."

I think different representatives interpret some of the categories differently.

### 5. Are there places where the current process is difficult, unclear, or requires workarounds?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

Categories are probably one of the biggest areas.

Escalations can also be confusing. Sometimes it's obvious that something needs to go to Compliance, but other times you're trying to decide whether it should go to a supervisor, another department, or Compliance.

The notes are pretty open-ended too. Some representatives write a lot and some write very little.

Branch referrals can be difficult. Sometimes a branch sends us everything we need. Other times it's basically, "This customer is upset, please call them," and then we have to start from the beginning to figure out what happened.

Repeat contacts are another confusing area. If a customer calls again about the same problem, I'm not always sure whether we should update the existing complaint or create another one. It's especially difficult if another representative handled the first contact.

### 6. Are there any data-quality issues you've noticed in complaint records?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

Categories and notes are probably the biggest ones.

Duplicates happen too. A customer might send a secure message and then call because they haven't gotten a response yet, and now two representatives may be looking at the same issue.

Branch information isn't always complete.

Product information can also be tricky if the issue involves more than one account or product.

And like I mentioned earlier, I'm sure there are complaints that never get entered because the representative fixed the issue during the interaction and moved on.

### 7. If the complaint process or reporting were changed, what would you want the people designing it to understand about your day-to-day work?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

Please don't give us another system to update.

We're already moving between customer information, account information, documentation, internal resources, other teams, and then trying to be ready for the next customer.

If you need us to capture more information, I think representatives need to understand why it's important.

I'd rather have clearer choices than more choices. If the categories are confusing, adding another 20 categories probably isn't going to help.

And if the system already knows something, it would be great if it could populate it automatically instead of making us enter it again.

### 8. What would success look like from your perspective?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

I'd want to know what I'm supposed to record without having to stop and think about whether another representative would make a different decision.

It should also be easy enough that documenting a complaint doesn't get in the way of actually helping the customer.

I'd like more visibility into what happens with complaints after we send them somewhere else. Sometimes you escalate something and don't really know what happens after that.

I'd also like the information we're collecting to actually result in problems getting fixed. If customers keep complaining about the same thing, it would be nice to know that someone is seeing that pattern.

And if the process were clearer, hopefully supervisors wouldn't need to come back weeks later and ask us what we meant in an old complaint record.

### 9. Is there anything else you think I should know or investigate?

**Senior Customer Service Representative:**

I'd talk to representatives who handle different types of customer contacts. Someone mostly answering calls might have a different experience from someone working secure messages or other channels.

I'd definitely look at repeat contacts. I'm not sure we're handling those consistently.

I'd also look at what happens after something gets escalated. From the representative side, that part of the process can feel like a black box.
