> **Simulation Note:** This is a fictional stakeholder review created for a portfolio project. I developed and presented the discovery findings based on my analysis of the simulated stakeholder interviews. The stakeholder responses to those findings were generated by ChatGPT based on the project scenario. I reviewed the simulated feedback and used it to identify clarifications and decisions that will be carried forward into requirements.

## Purpose

The discovery findings were presented back to key stakeholders to validate that the findings accurately represented the business problem and the perspectives gathered during discovery.

The review also provided an opportunity to identify areas requiring clarification before moving into requirements.

## Participants

- VP of Customer Experience
- Customer Service Manager
- Compliance Manager
- Digital Banking Product Manager
- Data Governance Lead
- BI Manager
- Retail Banking Operations Manager

## Findings Presented for Validation

The stakeholder group was presented with the refined problem statement and major findings documented in the [Discovery Findings](03-discovery-findings.md).

The primary shift from the original request was highlighted:

The original concern focused on complaint numbers reported by different departments not matching. Discovery indicated that matching departmental totals should not be the goal because different business areas legitimately capture different types of customer concerns for different purposes.

The larger problem is that Evergreen Community Bank lacks a shared enterprise understanding of what these different complaint and feedback metrics represent and how they should be interpreted together.

---

## Stakeholder Feedback

### VP of Customer Experience

The VP agreed that the refined problem statement more accurately represents the underlying business need.

The goal is not for Compliance, Customer Service, Digital Banking, and other areas to report identical numbers. Leadership needs to understand what those numbers represent and what they collectively indicate about the customer experience.

The VP also reinforced the value of incorporating broader customer signals, particularly when sources such as Digital Banking may identify an emerging issue before it becomes visible through traditional complaint reporting.

### Customer Service Manager

The Customer Service Manager agreed with the findings and emphasized that the future solution should preserve the operational purpose of Customer Service complaint records.

Customer Service should not be required to change its complaint process solely to make its reporting align with another department.

The manager also reinforced the importance of avoiding duplicate data entry or additional administrative work where possible.

### Compliance Manager

The Compliance Manager agreed with the overall findings and reinforced the distinction between enterprise reporting and Compliance case management.

One clarification was identified regarding privacy and access. Although Compliance may maintain some of the most restricted information, privacy and access controls may also apply to customer information from other sources.

Requirements should distinguish between aggregate information needed by leadership and more detailed information required by authorized operational users.

### Digital Banking Product Manager

The Digital Banking Product Manager agreed with the findings and supported keeping the relationship between customer feedback and formal complaints open for further definition.

Digital feedback should not automatically be treated as either a formal complaint or as information that falls outside complaint analysis.

The Product Manager also reinforced that feedback volume requires context. A large number of negative app reviews may represent an important customer signal without being equivalent to the same number of Customer Service complaint cases.

### Data Governance Lead

The Data Governance Lead agreed with the overall direction but recommended avoiding the assumption that the bank needs a single enterprise definition of a complaint.

Instead, the organization may need a shared **enterprise conceptual framework** that defines several related concepts and explains how they relate to one another.

The Data Governance Lead also reinforced the distinction between standardization and mapping. Departmental processes or categories should only be standardized where there is a business need. In other cases, mapping existing departmental concepts into broader enterprise concepts may be sufficient.

### BI Manager

The BI Manager agreed that understanding how departmental information and metrics relate conceptually is important.

However, the ability to connect individual records across systems should not currently be considered necessary for the success of the initial solution.

Record-level relationships may provide additional value, but their feasibility and usefulness need to be evaluated during data discovery. Historical records may not contain enough information to establish reliable relationships.

### Retail Banking Operations Manager

The Retail Banking Operations Manager agreed that the findings reflect the branch perspective.

The manager reinforced the importance of minimizing additional operational work and distinguishing between changes necessary for an initial solution and improvements that could be addressed through a longer-term roadmap.

The manager also emphasized that branch complaint counts should not be interpreted as performance measures without appropriate context.

## Clarifications and Decisions

Based on stakeholder feedback, the following clarifications will be carried forward into requirements.

### Enterprise complaint framework

The project will not assume that all customer concerns should be represented through a single standardized enterprise complaint definition.

Requirements will instead support the development of an enterprise conceptual framework that defines relevant complaint and customer feedback concepts and explains how departmental information contributes to the broader enterprise view.

### Departmental differences

Differences between departmental definitions, categories, processes, and complaint counts are not inherently data-quality problems.

Requirements should preserve legitimate operational differences while ensuring that users can understand and appropriately interpret the resulting information.

### Record-level relationships

Understanding how departmental metrics and concepts relate is necessary for the initial solution.

Connecting individual records across systems or associating multiple customer signals with the same underlying issue may provide additional value but is not currently considered a requirement for initial success.

The feasibility and value of these capabilities will be evaluated during data discovery.

### Privacy and access

Privacy and access requirements apply to sensitive information across the solution, not exclusively to Compliance data.

Requirements should account for different levels of access based on user needs and data sensitivity.

### Operational impact

The initial solution should minimize additional manual work and avoid requiring operational teams to duplicate information solely for reporting purposes.

Potential process or system improvements identified during later analysis should be evaluated based on business value and may be separated into initial and longer-term capabilities.

### Context for metrics

Complaint and feedback metrics should be presented with sufficient context to support appropriate interpretation.

This includes recognizing that departmental metrics may represent different populations and that raw counts may not provide meaningful comparisons in all situations, such as comparisons between branches with different levels or types of activity.

## Review Outcome

The stakeholder group agreed that the refined problem framing and discovery findings provide an appropriate foundation for requirements development.

The review confirmed that the goal of the initiative is not to force departmental complaint data into identical definitions, processes, or totals. The solution should instead provide a trustworthy enterprise framework for understanding different sources of customer complaints and feedback while preserving the operational context necessary to interpret them correctly.

The VP of Customer Experience approved moving forward into requirements development.

## Next Steps

The discovery findings and stakeholder review feedback will be used to develop the initial requirements for the solution.

Requirements will focus on the business outcomes and capabilities needed for success without prescribing a specific technical solution.

Following requirements development and validation, data discovery and profiling will be used to evaluate the current data against those requirements, validate stakeholder assumptions, identify gaps, and inform potential solution approaches.
