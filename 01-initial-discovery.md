# Initial Discovery

## Initial Request

The VP of Customer Experience requested a Power BI dashboard that would provide leadership with a reliable view of customer complaints. Existing complaint reporting is produced by multiple departments, and the reported numbers do not consistently align.

## Discovery Approach

The initial request proposed a Power BI dashboard as the solution. Before defining requirements or beginning development, I planned an initial discovery conversation with the VP of Customer Experience to better understand the underlying business problem and desired outcomes.

The questions were intentionally focused on the business context rather than technical or solution requirements. The goal was to determine why the initiative was needed, understand the current state at a high level, identify the appropriate stakeholders for deeper discovery, and establish how success should ultimately be evaluated.

Detailed process, data, and technical discovery would follow with the stakeholders closest to those areas.

## Prepared Discovery Questions

1. **What prompted this request?**
   Are you seeing evidence that customer complaints are increasing or changing, or is the primary concern that we don't currently have a reliable way to understand and act on customer feedback?

2. **What information do you have today?**
   Can you walk me through the reports or other sources you're currently using to understand customer complaints and, at a high level, how that information gets captured and reported today? What information does each provide, and where are you seeing inconsistencies?

3. **How is this information used today?**
   When leadership receives complaint information, what happens next? What kinds of decisions or actions does it currently inform?

4. **What do you need to understand about customer complaints that you can't reliably understand today?**
   What are the most important questions you want the eventual solution to answer?

5. **Who needs this information, and how might their needs differ?**
   Who uses complaint information today? Who do you expect would use a future solution, and what decisions would they need it to support?

6. **What would success look like?**
   If we can consistently answer the questions we've identified, is that sufficient for this initiative to be successful? Beyond answering those questions, are there other outcomes that would make this initiative successful?

7. **Who should be involved in discovery?**
   Which leaders, teams, subject-matter experts, and people performing the day-to-day work should I speak with? Who owns the relevant processes or data, and who could be impacted by changes?

8. **What should I know before moving forward?**
   Are there known constraints, risks, dependencies, previous attempts, regulatory considerations, deadlines, or other concerns that are top-of-mind?


## Simulated Stakeholder Interview

Because this is a fictional portfolio scenario, ChatGPT was used to simulate the VP of Customer Experience and respond to my prepared discovery questions. The notes below are the notes I took while reviewing the VP's simulated responses.

### What prompted this request?

- Leaders from Customer Service and Digital Banking said that complaints/negative feedback increased in the last six months, but numbers from Compliance did not match.
- There was a lack of understanding of how the numbers from the different departments relate to each other and what they say about the overall picture around complaints.
- We want to have visibility into complaints and have consistency so that everyone feels like they are on the same page.

### What information do you have today?

- Excel reports are created by Customer Service and Compliance for different purposes.
- **Customer Service** - Monthly - number of complaints they've received, complaint categories, how customers contacted us, which branch was involved when that's applicable, and whether the issue was resolved.
- **Compliance** - Complaint, regulatory category, when it was received, when it was resolved, and whether it required escalation.
- **Digital Banking** monitors app reviews and feedback submitted through the app. There is no set information gathered, but they will report on things that they think are concerning.
- Branches can receive complaints directly. Issues gathered should be sent to Customer Service, but there's a lack of visibility in this area.

### How is this information used today?

- The process is currently more reactive than proactive.
- Each department gives their information in different ways, and some of them only discuss issues as they see them.
- We don't have a process in place for looking into a complaint when it has been raised a certain number of times.
- The teams are also doing a lot of manual work to create reports.

### What do you need to understand about customer complaints that you can't reliably understand today?

- Are complaints increasing or decreasing?
- What are complaints about?
- Where are the complaints coming from?
- Are there certain products, channels, branches, or areas of the bank that are generating more problems?
- How long does it take to resolve issues?
- How often are we seeing certain complaints?
- What is the severity? Are they regulatory?

### Who needs this information, and how might their needs differ?

- **Senior Leadership** - Needs to see trends, significant issues, and areas needing immediate attention.
- **Customer Experience, Customer Service, Compliance, Retail Banking, and Digital Banking leadership** - Would be the ones looking deeper into the highest-priority issues to understand root cause.
- **Branch managers** - Only see complaints regarding their branch.
- **Compliance** might have sensitive information regarding their complaints that other leaders shouldn't see.

### What would success look like?

- Consistency in information; this should lead to more trust in conversations.
- Reduce manual time creating reports.
- We have a process in place for review and action so the dashboard is used and provides value.

### Who should be involved in discovery?

- **Customer Service Manager** - Owns their complaint process and monthly reporting.
- **Senior Customer Service Representatives** - Entering and handling complaints every day.
- **Compliance Manager** - Understand their complaint process and regulatory requirements.
- **Digital Banking Product Manager** - Digital feedback.
- **Retail Banking Operations Manager** - Represent the branch perspective and would probably be one of the larger consumers.
- **BI Manager** - Reporting and data.
- **Data Governance Lead** - Get an understanding of the past complaint standardization attempt.
- **Information Security** - Customer-level information might be needed and who would have access to it. Not needed in initial discovery.

### What should I know before moving forward?

- Don't want to make the same mistakes as the last attempt at standardization.
- There is not a shared definition of the word "complaint," which did cause issues in the past.
- Customer information might be sensitive.
- Don't add manual work.

## Next Steps

Based on the initial conversation with the VP of Customer Experience, the next step is to prepare for discovery conversations with the additional stakeholders identified during the interview. These conversations will help build a deeper understanding of the current complaint processes, reporting needs, data sources, definitions, constraints, and concerns across the organization.

Before conducting those interviews, I will complete a stakeholder analysis to clarify each stakeholder's role in the initiative, what I need to learn from them, potential impacts or considerations, and the appropriate priority for engaging them.
