# Customer Service Manager Interview Notes

## Does the current problem statement align with your understanding?

- Does not think Customer Service's numbers are wrong, but believes the different groups are not measuring complaints in the same way.
- Current Customer Service definition of a complaint: when a customer expresses dissatisfaction about a bank product, service, employee interaction, or experience and expects some kind of response or resolution.
- Complaints can be handled differently. For example, a representative might fix the customer's issue without formally logging the complaint.
- Need a better understanding of what each group's numbers actually represent.

## How are complaints received, categorized, tracked, resolved, and reported today?

- **Received:** Phone calls, secure messages, emails, referrals from branches, or forwarded from other departments.
- **Categories:** Fees, account servicing, employee/service experience, online or mobile banking, card issues, lending, a few others, and "Other."
- **Tracked:** When a complaint is identified, a record is created in the Customer Service system. Each complaint includes:
  - Category
  - Notes describing the issue
  - Customer and product, when applicable
  - Contact channel
  - Whether a branch was involved
- The representative who creates the complaint usually owns it. They will either resolve it or transfer it to the appropriate group.
- When the complaint is addressed, it is marked as resolved.
- Severity is not standardized.
- Representatives can escalate complaints or send them to Compliance.

## How is the monthly Customer Service report created?

- A supervisor exports complaint data to Excel monthly.
- Data cleanup is needed before the report can be finalized, including:
  - Correcting categories
  - Removing duplicates
  - Researching incomplete records
- Monthly totals and breakdowns are created and compared with previous months.
- Comments with additional details can be added to the report as needed.
- The whole process takes several hours per month.
- Customer Service gets asked why its numbers don't match other groups, which is frustrating and can be time-consuming to investigate.

## What definitions and business rules does Customer Service currently use?

- There is no documented decision tree for determining whether something is a complaint despite having a general definition.
- Complaints are sent to Compliance if someone alleges discrimination, fraud by an employee, unfair treatment related to lending, or certain other regulatory concerns.
- Complaints are escalated to Legal if someone threatens legal action.
- A complaint might relate to more than one issue, but the system requires one primary category to be selected.
- Complaint counts represent individual complaint records regardless of whether multiple complaints were submitted by the same customer.

## What are the biggest pain points or areas of manual work in the current process?

- Time spent on monthly reporting.
- Data inconsistency. Training occurs, but there can still be variation in how representatives handle and document complaints.
- The "Other" category is used more often than desired.
- Descriptions are sometimes incomplete.
- Some representatives don't log a complaint if they resolve the issue immediately, which impacts formal complaint tracking.
- There are inconsistencies in how branches send complaints to Customer Service. Some branches seem to have better processes than others.
- Matching and reconciling numbers with Compliance is time-consuming.
- Need to avoid solving the problem by asking representatives to enter the same information into an additional system.

## What would success look like from your perspective?

- Confidence that everyone is talking about the same thing when looking at complaint numbers, supported by clear and consistent definitions.
- Reduced manual cleanup and reporting.
- A solution that helps identify common or increasing complaints earlier so the organization can take action instead of being primarily reactive.
- Customer Service does not necessarily need all of Compliance's information, but there needs to be an understanding of how the groups' information relates.

## Are there any other considerations you think I should know about?

- Consider improvements to training so complaints are handled and documented more consistently.
- Avoid assuming that every piece of negative feedback needs to become a Customer Service complaint record.
- Include Customer Service representatives in discovery before deciding on a solution.
- Biggest concerns are clear definitions, consistent application, and not adding more work to the frontline process.

## Follow-Up: Negative Feedback and the Definition of a Complaint

### How should negative app-store feedback be treated?

I asked why negative app-store reviews would be treated differently from complaints and whether the distinction was primarily because the feedback was handled by a different department.

- The difference is not based on which department receives the feedback.
- Customer Service currently views a complaint as something that can be handled through its complaint process, which typically requires an identifiable customer and an issue that can be responded to or resolved.
- Negative app-store reviews can still provide valuable information about customer dissatisfaction even when they cannot be tied to a specific customer.
- Multiple negative reviews about the same issue could identify a broader or systemic problem even if the individual reviews never become Customer Service complaint cases.
- Leadership may need visibility into both formal complaints and broader negative feedback without necessarily treating them as equivalent.

### Can negative feedback become a complaint?

I asked whether negative feedback could eventually become a formal complaint.

- Negative feedback can become a formal Customer Service complaint if the customer can be identified and the issue requires an individual response or resolution.
- Feedback submitted through authenticated channels, such as online banking, could potentially be referred to Customer Service and handled through the existing complaint process.
- Anonymous app-store feedback is more difficult to process as an individual complaint because the bank may not be able to identify or contact the customer.
- Negative feedback may also identify a systemic issue that Digital Banking investigates without the individual feedback becoming a formal complaint case.

### Does knowing the customer's identity determine whether something is actually a complaint?

I challenged the current definition because a customer can express dissatisfaction through an app-store review, which appears to meet the stated definition of a complaint even when the customer cannot be identified.

- The manager acknowledged that the current definition may be combining two separate concepts:
  - **What constitutes a complaint**
  - **What constitutes a complaint that Customer Service can manage as an individual case**
- Customer identification is important to the current Customer Service workflow because complaints are handled as individual cases that are associated with a customer, investigated, and eventually marked as resolved.
- Not being able to identify the customer does not necessarily mean the feedback itself is not a complaint.
- An anonymous complaint may require a different type of resolution. Instead of resolving an individual customer's case, the bank may need to investigate and resolve the underlying product or service issue.
- The manager did not believe Customer Service should define this distinction for the entire bank and recommended involving Digital Banking and Compliance in the conversation.
