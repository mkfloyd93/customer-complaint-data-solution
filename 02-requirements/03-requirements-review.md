# Requirements Review

## Purpose

This document records stakeholder review of the proposed business and solution requirements for the Customer Complaint Intelligence & Reporting Solution.

The review was conducted to validate that the requirements accurately reflect the business outcomes, reporting needs, data expectations, governance responsibilities, security considerations, and operational constraints identified during discovery.

The requirements presented for review are documented in:

- [Business Requirements](01-business-requirements.md)
- [Solution Requirements](02-solution-requirements.md)

The original requirements documents are retained as presented to preserve the project decision history. Clarifications and approved changes resulting from this review are documented below and will be carried forward into data discovery and solution design.

# Stakeholder Feedback and Decisions

## VP of Customer Experience

### Feedback

The VP agreed that the requirements better address the underlying business problem than the original request for a dashboard.

She confirmed that leadership needs to understand changes in complaint and feedback activity and quickly identify areas requiring attention.

She also emphasized that leadership reporting should remain understandable and should not become unnecessarily complicated by the differences between departmental processes.

The VP supported reducing manual reporting effort and recommended establishing a current-state baseline so improvement can eventually be measured.

### Decision

**No change was made to the requirements regarding leadership understanding of departmental concepts.**

While reporting should present information clearly, leadership must understand the relevant distinctions between departmental complaint and feedback concepts in order to interpret enterprise reporting appropriately.

Removing this context could recreate the original reporting problem by encouraging comparisons between metrics that represent different populations or business concepts.

Existing requirements FR-01, FR-02, FR-08, and NFR-06 address the need to provide appropriate definitions, context, and guidance.

The current manual reporting effort will be further assessed during current-state and data discovery activities to support evaluation of BR-05 and NFR-01.

---

## Customer Service Manager

### Feedback

The Customer Service Manager supported maintaining the operational meaning of Customer Service complaint information rather than requiring alignment with Compliance or other business areas.

She emphasized that enterprise mappings should not replace or obscure the original Customer Service categories used for operational purposes.

She also expressed interest in understanding whether complaint records can be followed after referral or escalation to another business area, particularly Compliance.

### Decision

**No requirement change was needed regarding preservation of departmental information.**

DR-04 already requires enterprise mappings to preserve meaningful departmental distinctions.

The ability to identify individual referrals or escalations across systems remains subject to data discovery. The project will investigate available handoff and escalation data without making historical cross-system matching a requirement for initial solution success.

---

## Compliance Manager

### Feedback

The Compliance Manager reinforced that sensitive information should not be included in enterprise reporting simply because it is available in a source system.

She also clarified that a concern does not need to become a Customer Service complaint before it can become relevant to Compliance.

Compliance metrics must retain sufficient context so leadership does not interpret Compliance cases as directly equivalent to Customer Service complaint cases.

### Decision

**Existing security requirements were considered sufficient.**

GPR-06 through GPR-08 address sensitive data identification, role-appropriate access, and minimum necessary data exposure.

The interpretation requirement is also addressed through FR-02 and FR-08.

The project will carry forward the clarification that escalation or referral to Compliance does not depend on a concern first qualifying as a Customer Service complaint. Handoff and escalation rules identified during subsequent analysis must preserve the independent responsibilities and definitions of participating business areas.

---

## Digital Banking Product Manager

### Feedback

The Digital Banking Product Manager supported preserving the distinction between digital feedback signals and formal complaint cases.

She noted that raw digital feedback volume may be misleading without appropriate context. Changes in customer population, application usage, releases, or feedback volume could influence counts independently of changes in customer experience.

### Decision

**No new reporting requirement was added at this stage.**

FR-08 already requires sufficient context to prevent misleading interpretation of complaint and feedback metrics.

Data discovery will investigate what contextual information is available and appropriate for interpreting digital feedback volumes. Normalization or other adjustments will not be required until the available data and business value are better understood.

---

## Data Governance Lead

### Feedback

The Data Governance Lead supported the ownership and change-governance requirements but clarified that approval responsibilities may differ across concepts. The project should not assume that every department leader must approve every definition or mapping.

She also identified a need to preserve the meaning of historical reporting when definitions, categories, or mappings change over time.

For example, replacing an existing category definition or mapping without retaining sufficient historical context could cause previous reporting periods to be interpreted using rules that did not apply at the time.

### Decision

**The requirements were expanded to explicitly require historical interpretability.**

The following clarification is added to **GPR-04 — Change Governance**:

> Changes to complaint and feedback definitions, categories, mappings, and related business rules must be managed in a way that preserves appropriate interpretation of historical reporting.

The specific implementation method is not prescribed by the requirement.

Potential approaches such as effective dating, versioned mappings, or change-history tracking will be evaluated during solution design.

Approval authority will be determined through the governance model rather than requiring unanimous approval from all participating department leaders.

---

## Retail Banking Operations Manager

### Feedback

The Retail Banking Operations Manager supported requirements intended to minimize duplicate entry and additional operational burden.

She reinforced that raw complaint counts should not be used to make unsupported comparisons between branches because branch size, transaction volume, customer population, and operating context may differ.

Branch managers would still benefit from understanding complaint and feedback trends relevant to their locations.

### Decision

**No new requirement was added.**

FR-08 already requires appropriate context and prohibits reporting designs that imply unsupported conclusions from raw comparisons.

Data discovery will investigate what contextual information is available to support meaningful branch-level analysis.

---

## BI Manager

### Feedback

The BI Manager confirmed that the requirements provide sufficient direction to begin data discovery without prematurely prescribing an architecture.

She supported treating gaps in source availability, reliability, and repeatability as current-state gaps rather than weakening the eventual solution requirements.

The BI Manager also identified reporting currency as an unresolved requirement. The frequency with which leadership needs updated information could materially affect solution options and should therefore be clarified before solution design.

### Follow-Up with VP of Customer Experience

The VP clarified that monthly reporting is sufficient as the minimum leadership reporting cadence.

More current information could provide value when complaint or feedback activity changes rapidly, but the VP did not identify real-time reporting as a business requirement.

More frequent reporting should therefore be considered where it provides meaningful business value and can be supported sustainably.

### Decision

**A reporting currency requirement was added.**

### NFR-07 — Reporting Currency

**Supports:** BR-04, BR-05

Enterprise complaint and feedback reporting must support at least a monthly reporting cadence for leadership.

More frequent data availability may be considered where it provides meaningful business value and can be supported sustainably.

The technical refresh mechanism and source-specific refresh schedules will be determined during solution evaluation and design.

---

# Approved Requirement Changes

The stakeholder review resulted in two substantive changes to the proposed solution requirements.

### GPR-04 — Change Governance

The following requirement is added to the existing change-governance requirement:

> Changes to complaint and feedback definitions, categories, mappings, and related business rules must be managed in a way that preserves appropriate interpretation of historical reporting.

The method used to preserve historical interpretation will be determined during solution design.

### NFR-07 — Reporting Currency

The following requirement is added:

> Enterprise complaint and feedback reporting must support at least a monthly reporting cadence for leadership.
>
> More frequent data availability may be considered where it provides meaningful business value and can be supported sustainably.

---

# Additional Clarifications

The review also resulted in the following clarifications without requiring additional requirements:

- Leadership is expected to understand relevant differences between departmental complaint and feedback concepts in order to appropriately interpret enterprise reporting.
- Enterprise mappings must preserve original departmental meaning and must not replace operational categories.
- A concern does not need to qualify as a Customer Service complaint before it can be referred or escalated to Compliance.
- Sensitive information should not be included in enterprise reporting when it is unnecessary to satisfy the reporting need.
- Raw departmental, digital, or branch complaint volumes should not be interpreted without appropriate business context.
- Historical record-level relationships between systems remain an area for investigation rather than a requirement for initial solution success.
- Reporting frequency beyond the monthly leadership requirement will be evaluated based on business value, source capabilities, and sustainability.

---

# Review Outcome

Stakeholders agreed that the requirements provide an appropriate foundation for proceeding into data discovery.

The review confirmed that the solution should provide a trusted enterprise understanding of complaint and feedback activity while preserving legitimate differences between departmental concepts, definitions, and operational processes.

The approved requirements do not require all departmental metrics to align, all records to be connected across systems, or all operational processes to be standardized.

The original requirements documents are retained as the requirements presented for review. The changes and clarifications documented in this review represent the approved requirements moving forward.

## Next Steps

The project will proceed into data discovery and profiling.

Data discovery will evaluate the available Customer Service, Compliance, Digital Banking, Retail Banking, and relevant enterprise reference data against the approved requirements.

The analysis will focus on:

- Available fields and data structures
- Data completeness and quality
- Category values and definitions
- Available identifiers and relationships
- Handoff and escalation information
- Sensitive data elements
- Historical coverage
- Available contextual measures
- Source availability and reporting cadence
- Gaps between current-state data and approved requirements

Findings from data discovery will be used to identify requirement gaps, evaluate potential solution approaches, and inform the solution design.
